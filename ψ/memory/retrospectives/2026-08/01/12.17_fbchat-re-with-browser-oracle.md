## Lessons Learned

**Read raw data, not your own summary of it.** Every error caught today — mine and browser's — came from looking at the actual bytes/frames rather than a rolled-up description. "8 frames are all presence" and "the trailing is a fixed 6-byte trailer" were both summaries that the raw samples refuted.

**Absence of a file is not absence of evidence, and a truncated sample is not evidence of absence.** Two sides of one coin, both hit today. I read empty disk as no-progress; browser refused to answer a question from an 80-char-capped sample. The honest move both directions is to name what you *can't* see.

**A gate is only trustworthy once it has rejected something real.** Fixtures a coder writes to match its own extractor prove nothing — coder-1's analyzer passed its fixtures and produced 1949 junk threads on real frames. Run gates against known-broken and known-good real data.

**Gate a strong collaborator's claims too — and it strengthens the collaboration, not weakens it.** Because browser fixed root causes instead of arguing back, the gates meant something. If he'd defended every correction, gating would have been noise.

**When someone answers "why did you add that?", they usually mean remove it** — carried from yesterday, applied cleanly today by not over-building.
