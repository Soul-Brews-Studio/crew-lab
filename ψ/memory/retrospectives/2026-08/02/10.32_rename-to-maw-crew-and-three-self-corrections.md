## Honest Feedback

**The measuring layer corrupted output three separate times.** `diff <(cat a) <(cat b)` reported
identical files as identical when md5 proved otherwise. `rg` on a single file printed "5 matches
in 5 files", which is not a coherent thing for a one-file search to say. And `RC=${PIPESTATUS[0]}`
came back **empty** on the two most important commands of the session — `crew up` and `crew down`
— so I never actually read their exit codes and had to verify state independently. I got the right
answers, but not from the instruments I reached for first.

**Two hook/transport rejections cost round-trips.** `git branch -D` is blocked by safety-check.sh,
so both stale branches needed `git update-ref -d` instead. And the first `maw hey` bounced with
"bracket-prefixed hey text is reserved for signed transport prefixes" because I opened with
`[proposal:crew-lab]`. Both are correct guards; both were discovered by tripping them.

**`maw ls` reports windows as panes.** It said "2 panes" for a session with two single-pane
windows, which is what sent Nat asking whether crews should be tabs or panes. The confusion was
manufactured entirely by the display, and I initially attributed it to our plugin before
maw-crew pointed out it is maw's own listing.

## Lessons Learned

**A confirmed premise is permission to evaluate, not permission to ship.** maw-crew verified my
isolation claim at source and declined the change anyway, on teardown risk and repo ownership —
neither of which the premise touched. Verifying a fact and approving a change are separate
questions; merging them is how a correct fact becomes a bad build.

**A keyword match landing near your hypothesis is a lying instrument.** `grep session` returns
`SessionStart` — a codex hook-event, unrelated to tmux — and it reads as evidence *for*
session-coupling when the truth is the opposite. Confirmation-shaped noise is the most expensive
kind, because it stops the search exactly when the search was wrong. A search hit is a pointer to
read, never a finding.

**Content hash beats diff-of-cat.** When two observations contradict, prefer the measurement with
the fewest layers between you and the bytes.

**A cross-repo pointer is a perishable good.** Documentation that points at another repo does not
inherit an exemption from that repo's changes. Re-check it when the target moves — or it becomes
the stale-fork trap it was written to prevent.

**Check who can own a change before proposing it.** My proposal was structurally un-ownable by
its recipient, because the behaviour originated in a third repo. Good measurement does not rescue
a change addressed to someone who cannot land it.

**"No" is a complete outcome — record it as decided, with a re-open trigger.** A declined proposal
left in a pending list gets re-litigated every recap.
